I was watching Angels & Demons when something small caught my attention.

A page titled “Diagramma della Verità” (Diagram of Truth), attributed to Galileo Galilei, appeared on screen. Old paper. Classical typography. And at the bottom… a tree with a vine.

Suddenly, it felt familiar.

That symbol is almost identical to the logo of Elsevier.

At first glance, it looks like just a decorative engraving. But it carries a deeper meaning.

The tree represents knowledge. The vine represents growth. And the idea behind it is simple: knowledge does not grow in isolation.

Elsevier’s motto says it clearly: Non Solus — “Not alone”.

What’s interesting is how a modern scientific publisher and a fictional historical document in the film both use the same symbolic language.

Because here’s the key detail:

The “Diagramma della Verità” shown in the movie is not an actual historical document written by Galileo. It is a fictional element created for storytelling — but designed using real Renaissance-style symbolism.

That imagery comes from a much older tradition — the culture of early scientific publishing, where knowledge, collaboration, and shared discovery were central ideas.

The film uses it to create authenticity. Elsevier uses it to represent its mission.

Same symbol. Same idea. Different contexts. And once you notice it, you start seeing it everywhere.
